> # QUESTION:  When an AI tool explains a compiler error, what evidence should you use to decide whether its explanation is correct?

# MY ANSWER:

---

When an AI tool explains a compiler error, it can often sound very confident while simultaneously being very wrong. So, how can you determine if the answer is accurate or not?

One compiler error I personally ran into was a missing semicolon line terminator. Another one is an undefined variable. I've ran into those many times in my career as a web developer. Therefore, the first evidence I use is what I see on the screen and my experience with that error. For the missing semicolon error, thats a pretty easy one because the error messages points to the exact line where it broke down.

However, with more complex errors, AI can be real help in explaining what the error means and how to solve it. But, for complex errors I've never seen, I need to verify the AI is giving me correct information. Often I find reviewing the sources it used very helpful. Also, just Googling the answer and ignoring the AI generated answer at the top but instead examining the regular search results is good way to find out the correct information.

Finally, using well-established sources like StackOverflow, WikiPedia, Reddit, and BleepingComputer can help validate an AI's answers.

In conclusion, I use evidence from a variety of sources to corroborate or invalidate the AI's response including established tech websites and my own experience in the field.
